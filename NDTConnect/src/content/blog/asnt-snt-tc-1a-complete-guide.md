# ASNT SNT-TC-1A Complete Guide for Inspection Companies

If you run an NDT department or an inspection shop in the United States, SNT-TC-1A is either the spine of your personnel program or the document people pretend exists when an auditor walks in. There is no middle ground. This guide is for the people who want it to be the spine - written by working Level IIIs for working Level IIIs.

## What SNT-TC-1A Is, and What It Is Not

SNT-TC-1A is a **recommended practice** published by the American Society for Nondestructive Testing. The current edition is the 2020 edition (with errata); a new edition is balloted on a multi-year cycle. Verify your written practice cites the edition the customer's contract requires. Most North American refining and structural contracts will accept the 2020 edition; some legacy contracts still reference 2011 or 2016.

Three things SNT-TC-1A is not:

- **Not a standard.** Unlike ANSI/ASNT CP-189, SNT-TC-1A is a guideline. The numbers in it are recommendations, not absolutes. The employer's written practice can adjust them with documented technical justification.
- **Not a certification.** SNT-TC-1A does not certify anyone. The employer certifies. ASNT publishes the recommended practice; ASNT separately certifies Level IIIs through its own program.
- **Not a substitute for the Level III's judgment.** The document explicitly delegates technical authority to the employer's Level III. A weak or absent Level III voids the program regardless of paperwork.

Internalize all three before writing a written practice. The most common audit failures trace back to misunderstanding one of them.

## The Written Practice: Your Operating Document

SNT-TC-1A paragraph 5 requires the employer to establish a **written practice** for control and administration of personnel training, examination, and certification. This document, signed by a Level III, is the deliverable that auditors will read.

A defensible written practice covers:

### 1. Scope and applicable methods

Each method (UT, MT, PT, RT, ET, VT, LT, NR, AE, etc.) explicitly listed, with technique sub-categories where relevant (e.g., "UT including PAUT and TOFD per qualified procedures"). Methods not on the list cannot be performed under the program.

### 2. Levels of qualification

Levels I, II, III defined per SNT-TC-1A paragraph 6. Trainee status defined. Limited Level certifications (where used) explicitly described.

### 3. Education, training, and experience requirements

The recommendation tables in SNT-TC-1A paragraph 8 are the starting point. The 2020 edition lists, for example:

- UT Level I: 40 hours formal training, 210 hours experience minimum
- UT Level II: additional 40 hours formal training, 630 hours additional experience
- RT Level I: 40 hours formal training, 210 hours experience
- RT Level II: additional 40 hours formal training, 630 hours additional experience
- MT Level I: 12 hours formal training, 70 hours experience
- MT Level II: additional 8 hours formal training, 140 hours additional experience
- PT Level I: 4 hours formal training, 70 hours experience
- PT Level II: additional 8 hours formal training, 140 hours additional experience

Verify the exact numbers against the edition cited in your written practice. The numbers shift between editions and between high-school-graduate vs. post-secondary entry paths.

A written practice that copies the table verbatim is acceptable. One that reduces the hours without documented technical justification is not.

### 4. Examination requirements

Per SNT-TC-1A paragraph 9, examinations include:

- **General examination**: closed-book, principles of the method, minimum 40 questions for Level I/II
- **Specific examination**: open-book where it covers procedures, equipment, and acceptance criteria specific to the employer
- **Practical examination**: hands-on demonstration on representative specimens, scored against known indications

Composite passing grade typically 80%, with no individual exam below 70%. The written practice can tighten this; it cannot loosen it without justification.

The specific examination is where employer-specific written procedures and acceptance criteria are tested. A common audit finding is a generic specific exam not tied to the employer's actual procedures.

### 5. Vision examination

Annual near-vision (Jaeger 2 at 12 inches or equivalent) and color test at certification and recertification. Records retained for the life of the certification.

### 6. Certification and recertification

Levels I and II: typically 3-5 year recertification per the written practice, by re-examination or documented continuing proficiency.

Level III: typically 5 years, by re-examination, structured points (similar to ACCP), or recurrent training and demonstration. The Level III must hold an external ASNT Level III credential or equivalent unless the written practice grandfathers an internal route - rarely defensible in 2026.

### 7. Suspension, revocation, and reinstatement

Continuity of certification: a documented break in active work in the method exceeding 12 months (typical) requires re-examination. The written practice must define what constitutes a break and what reinstatement requires.

### 8. Records

The records retained per certified individual:

- Name and identification
- Level, method, technique
- Educational background and experience
- Statement of physical condition (vision, color)
- Training hours by method, by level, with dates and instructors
- Examination records (general, specific, practical) with scores and dates
- Other appropriate evidence of qualification
- Date of certification and expiration
- Signature of the Level III certifying

A central register of all of these per technician is the operational requirement. Spreadsheets work for shops under five technicians; beyond that, a real system is required for audit retrieval.

## Level III Responsibilities

SNT-TC-1A paragraph 6.3 specifies the Level III's role. In practice, this person is responsible for:

- Establishing and maintaining the written practice
- Approving NDT procedures, written practices, and techniques
- Establishing and conducting training, examinations, and certifications
- Auditing outside-agency qualifications when used
- Acting as the technical contact for customers and auditors
- Interpreting codes, standards, specifications, and procedures

A single Level III can be responsible for multiple methods. The same person can be the contracting officer; cannot also be the customer's representative on the same job. Conflict of interest matters.

The Level III's signature is the final attestation on the written practice, on every certification, and on every procedure revision. If the Level III leaves, the program does not lapse - but a new Level III must be appointed and must review and re-attest within a defined window (typically 30-90 days, per the written practice).

## Customer Flow-Down: How Contracts Translate

A typical refinery PO clause: "All NDT shall be performed by personnel certified in accordance with SNT-TC-1A latest edition under an approved written practice."

Translated:

- Your written practice must reference the latest edition (or the customer must accept a deviation in writing)
- Personnel must be certified per that practice in the methods called out
- The written practice must be available to the customer on request
- The Level III appointing the personnel must be identified

A more rigorous PO clause: "All NDT shall be performed in accordance with ANSI/ASNT CP-189."

Translated: SNT-TC-1A is not sufficient. CP-189 is more prescriptive (more required hours, mandatory written examination by the certification authority, additional Level III audit requirements). A program written to SNT-TC-1A will fail if the contract calls for CP-189.

Aerospace flow-down typically calls for NAS 410 in addition to or instead of SNT-TC-1A. Power generation and nuclear flow-down typically calls for ASME Section XI Appendix VII for personnel performance demonstration on top of SNT-TC-1A or CP-189.

## SNT-TC-1A vs CP-189 vs ACCP

| Aspect | SNT-TC-1A | CP-189 | ACCP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Recommended practice | Standard (ANSI) | Central certification |
| Certifying party | Employer | Employer | ASNT (third party) |
| Training hours | Recommended, employer can adjust | Mandatory | Mandatory |
| Examination | Employer-administered | Employer-administered, with constraints | ASNT-administered |
| Practical exam | Employer | Employer | ASNT-approved examiner |
| Recertification | Employer-defined | Standardized | 5 yr renewal, 10 yr recert |
| Used by | Most US refining, structural | ASME-referenced work, some refining | Suppliers needing third-party cert |

The simplest decision rule: write to SNT-TC-1A by default. Write to CP-189 if any contract requires it. Hold ACCP credentials in addition where customers require third-party certification.

## Common Audit Findings on SNT-TC-1A Programs

After several hundred audits across refining, aerospace, and structural shops, the recurring findings cluster:

### 1. Written practice references an obsolete edition

The written practice cites SNT-TC-1A 2011 while the customer contract says "latest edition." Updating the written practice and re-attesting is straightforward but routinely missed.

### 2. Specific exam not employer-specific

A purchased specific exam that tests generic UT theory rather than the employer's actual written procedures and acceptance criteria.

### 3. Practical exam records missing the specimen ID

The practical was performed but the specimen used (and its known indications) is not documented. The exam is unauditable.

### 4. Vision test gaps

Annual vision test missed by weeks or months. The certification is technically void during the gap. A NADCAP-equivalent finding.

### 5. Continuity broken without re-exam

Technician returned after 14 months without certified work in the method. Re-exam required but not performed.

### 6. Level III appointment letter missing

The Level III is functioning - approving procedures, signing certifications - but the formal appointment letter from management is not in the file.

### 7. Outside-agency qualifications not audited

A subcontracted technician's certifications accepted at face value without the employer's Level III auditing the outside agency's program.

### 8. Training records do not match certification dates

The technician was certified before the recommended training hours were complete, with no documented technical justification.

Each of these is preventable with a real personnel-record system. None is preventable with a binder.

## Operational Patterns That Pass Audits

Three habits separate clean SNT-TC-1A programs from compliance theater:

### 1. The Level III owns the written practice as a living document

Quarterly review at minimum, with revision history. New methods, new equipment, new customers all trigger updates. The Level III's name and signature on every revision.

### 2. Certifications expire on a calendar, not in someone's head

A central register with expiry dates and tiered alerts. The dispatcher cannot assign a technician to work in a method where the certification expired yesterday.

### 3. Audit retrieval under 60 seconds per technician

When the auditor names a technician and a method, the complete certification dossier (training, exam, vision, practical, written-practice acknowledgment, recertification history) appears in under a minute. Shops that hit this consistently pass NADCAP, AS9100, and API audits with minor or no findings on personnel.

## Building the Written Practice From Zero

For a new shop or a shop replacing an inherited practice, a defensible build sequence:

1. **Identify the methods** the company actually performs. Do not list aspirational methods.
2. **Choose the SNT-TC-1A edition** the customer base requires. Default to the most recent edition.
3. **Adopt the recommended training and experience tables** unmodified for the first revision. Tighten in revision 2 if needed; never loosen without documented justification.
4. **Write method-specific specific examinations** tied to your actual procedures and equipment. Do not buy generic specific exams.
5. **Document the practical exam specimens** with photographs, sketches, and known-indication maps.
6. **Appoint the Level III** in writing, with management signature.
7. **Audit the first three certifications** end-to-end before issuing them, to validate the process.
8. **Set the recertification calendar** at the time of certification, not the day before expiry.

This sequence produces a program that passes audit on the first review and survives Level III turnover.

## Records: The Backbone

Every clause of SNT-TC-1A points back to records. The program is, in the end, a records system attached to a Level III's judgment. The records must be:

- **Complete**: every required field populated for every certified person
- **Retrievable**: indexed by name, method, level, expiry
- **Traceable**: every change attributed to a person and a date
- **Retainable**: stored for the life of the certification plus contract retention (typically 7 years post-employment for refining, longer for aerospace and nuclear)
- **Exportable**: producible to an auditor or a customer in a structured format on demand

A binder fails on retrievability and traceability. A spreadsheet fails on traceability. A real system, free or paid, fails on none of these.
