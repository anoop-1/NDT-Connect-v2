# NDT Certifications Explained: ASNT, ISO 9712, PCN, NAS 410, CGSB, NADCAP

The single most-confused topic in our industry is what "certified" means. A Level II UT card from one scheme is not the same legal instrument as a Level II UT card from another. Customer flow-down clauses that say "ASNT certified" are routinely misapplied, and shops bid work they cannot legally execute because they confused employer-based certification with third-party. This guide cuts through that.

## The Three ASNT Documents That Are Not the Same Thing

The American Society for Nondestructive Testing publishes three personnel-related documents, each with a different legal character:

### ANSI/ASNT CP-189: Standard for Qualification and Certification of NDT Personnel

This is a **standard**. When invoked by contract or by a quality system, it is mandatory and prescriptive. Levels I/II/III are issued by the employer, but training hours, exam composition, and Level III responsibilities are non-negotiable. CP-189 is referenced by ASME Section V Article 1 T-120(e) and Article 23 for boiler and pressure vessel work. It is more rigorous than SNT-TC-1A.

### SNT-TC-1A: Recommended Practice for the Qualification and Certification of NDT Personnel

This is a **recommended practice**, not a standard. The employer adapts it to their own written practice. Training hours, exam content, and recertification cycles are guidelines that the employer can tighten or loosen with technical justification. The flexibility is the point - and the pitfall. A poorly written employer practice can make SNT-TC-1A a paper exercise. A well-written one, with a competent Level III, exceeds CP-189. The 2020 edition is current at this writing; verify your written practice cites the correct edition.

### ASNT NDT Level III Certification

A **personal** certification issued by ASNT after a candidate passes a Basic exam plus a method exam. Holding an ASNT Level III does not automatically make you the company Level III - the employer must still appoint and document the person under their written practice. The card is portable; the authority is not.

### ACCP: ASNT Central Certification Program

A **central** certification scheme run by ASNT, equivalent in structure to ISO 9712 third-party schemes. Practical exam, general exam, specific exam, vision test, all administered through ASNT. Far less common in the US than employer-based SNT-TC-1A; common in supplier qualifications where third-party certification is required.

## ISO 9712: The International Third-Party Standard

ISO 9712 (Non-destructive testing - Qualification and certification of NDT personnel) is the global third-party scheme. Levels 1/2/3, examinations administered by an authorized certification body (in the US, that body is ASNT through ACCP; in the UK, BINDT through PCN; in Canada, NRCan through CGSB).

Key differences from SNT-TC-1A:

- Certification is by the certification body, not the employer
- Renewal every 5 years, recertification every 10 years (with structured credit)
- Practical exam is mandatory and externally administered
- Sector qualifications (welds, castings, forgings, tubes, etc.) are explicit

ISO 9712 is the default scheme in Europe, the Gulf, and most of Asia. US shops bidding into Aramco, ADNOC, and most European operators encounter ISO 9712 flow-down rather than SNT-TC-1A.

## PCN, CGSB, AINDT: The National Schemes

### PCN (Personnel Certification in Non-destructive Testing) - UK

Operated by BINDT under ISO 9712. Levels 1/2/3, sectors include weldments, castings, forgings, wrought, pre-service, in-service. PCN is heavily flow-down in oil and gas and aerospace; UK MOD work effectively requires it.

### CGSB (Canadian General Standards Board) - Canada

CAN/CGSB-48.9712 mirrors ISO 9712. Administered by NRCan. CGSB certifications are required for federal nuclear and pipeline work in Canada (CSA Z662, CNSC).

### AINDT - Australia

The Australian Institute for NDT operates an ISO 9712-compliant scheme. Required across most Australian heavy industry under AS 3669.

These three (and a handful of equivalents in Brazil ABENDI, Japan JSNDI, etc.) interoperate via ISO 9712 mutual recognition where it exists.

## Aerospace: NAS 410, EN 4179, and NADCAP

### NAS 410: NAS Certification & Qualification of Nondestructive Test Personnel

The North American aerospace scheme. Issued by AIA (Aerospace Industries Association). Employer-based like SNT-TC-1A, but with mandatory training hours, examination requirements, and practical demonstrations that exceed SNT-TC-1A. NAS 410 Revision 5 is current; verify your written practice references the correct revision.

NAS 410 highlights:

- Minimum training hours per method per level are non-negotiable (e.g., 40 hours UT Level II initial, after 16 hours Level I)
- Outside Agency Approval permitted but the employer remains responsible
- Vision exam annual (Jaeger 2 at 16 in or equivalent), color test at certification and recertification
- Renewal every 3 years for I/II, every 5 years for III, with practical demonstration

### EN 4179: Aerospace - Qualification and Approval of Personnel for NDT

The European aerospace equivalent. NAS 410 and EN 4179 are technically aligned and accept reciprocal training hours. Boeing, Airbus, and the major engine OEMs accept either.

### NADCAP NDT (AC7114 series)

NADCAP (National Aerospace and Defense Contractors Accreditation Program), administered by the Performance Review Institute (PRI), audits aerospace NDT suppliers against AC7114 baseline plus method-specific checklists (AC7114/1 PT, /2 MT, /3 UT, /4 RT, /5 ET, /9 thermography). Audits are roughly annual once accredited; loss of NADCAP NDT accreditation generally costs the supplier its prime aerospace customers within months.

NADCAP audit findings cluster on:

- Personnel files missing one of: training records, exam records, vision tests, practical demonstrations, current certification
- Procedure revisions not flowed to the work area
- Calibration records gaps on standards, blocks, and densitometers
- Written practice not approved by a current Level III

The personnel-file finding is the most frequent. A NADCAP-accredited shop must produce, within minutes, the complete certification dossier for any technician on the audit floor.

## Levels I, II, and III: What Each Actually Authorizes

### Level I

Performs specific calibrations, specific tests, and records results per written instructions. Does not interpret or evaluate against acceptance criteria, does not write procedures.

### Level II

Sets up equipment, calibrates, performs and interprets tests against codes, standards, and procedures. Can train and guide Level I. Reports results.

### Level III

Develops and approves procedures, written practices, training programs. Interprets codes and standards. Evaluates the adequacy of techniques. Designates and verifies acceptance criteria where not specified by code or contract. Oversees Level I and II certification.

In SNT-TC-1A and CP-189 schemes, Level III is the technical and legal anchor. The Level III's signature on the written practice is what makes the entire program valid.

## Renewal, Recertification, and Continuity

| Scheme | Recertification interval | Renewal mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| SNT-TC-1A I/II | Per employer practice, typically 3-5 yr | Re-exam or documented proficiency |
| SNT-TC-1A III | Typically 5 yr | Re-exam, point system, or recurrent training |
| CP-189 I/II | 5 yr | Re-exam |
| CP-189 III | 5 yr | Re-exam or structured points |
| ISO 9712 | 5 yr renewal, 10 yr recertification | Structured credit / re-exam at 10 yr |
| NAS 410 I/II | 3 yr | Re-exam + practical |
| NAS 410 III | 5 yr | Re-exam + practical or point system |
| ACCP | 5 yr | Per ISO 9712 |
| PCN | 5 yr renewal, 10 yr recert | Per ISO 9712 |

Continuity of certification (no break exceeding the scheme's allowance, typically 12 months for SNT-TC-1A, 6-12 for ISO 9712) is the frequent gap. A technician returning after 14 months without certified work needs full re-examination.

## Exam Structure

Standard for SNT-TC-1A, CP-189, NAS 410:

- **General**: closed-book, principles of the method
- **Specific**: open-book, employer's procedures and equipment
- **Practical**: hands-on demonstration, real specimens, with a certified Level III observing

ISO 9712 is harder on practical: timed, scored against specimens with known indications, externally administered.

## Customer Flow-Down Reality

A purchase order that says "all NDT personnel shall be ASNT certified" is technically vague - ASNT does not directly certify Level I and II under SNT-TC-1A. The phrase usually means "certified per your SNT-TC-1A written practice with a Level III holding ASNT Level III credentials."

PO clauses that explicitly require ISO 9712, PCN, or NAS 410 are not interchangeable with SNT-TC-1A. If the contract calls for ISO 9712, you cannot perform the work with SNT-TC-1A personnel even if your Level III thinks the practice is equivalent.

## Where Certification Programs Fail

Three failure modes account for nearly all audit findings:

1. **Continuity broken.** Technician's last documented work in the method is older than the scheme allows. Solution: every job logged against the technician.
2. **Vision tests expired.** Annual near-vision missed by a month. Trivially preventable; routinely cited.
3. **Records not retrievable.** The certifications exist but cannot be produced in the audit window.

A central certification register, with expiry dates, vision-test dates, training records, and exam records all linked to the technician and the method, eliminates all three.
